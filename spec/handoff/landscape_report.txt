

    
    
    
    
    Landscape of LiDAR-to-Semantic Spatial IR and World Compilers

    
    Executive summary

    The strongest conclusion from the literature and product landscape is that no single organization currently appears to implement the complete architecture we have been defining:

    raw observations→multiscale geometry→structured reconstruction→semantic/topological world graph→theme/geometry/material compiler→runtime targets

    with the additional closed loop

    world model→simulated LiDAR→comparison with original observations.

    Instead, the components exist separately, often at a very high level of maturity. The closest semantic/world-graph architecture I found is MIT SPARK's Hydra + Spark-DSG, which incrementally converts sensor observations into hierarchical 3D scene graphs containing places, objects, rooms/buildings, geometry, and relationships. [1] The closest city-scale airborne reconstruction work is TU Delft's City3D/3D BAG/3dfier ecosystem and ETH Zurich's Point2Building, which turn airborne LiDAR and GIS into structured building geometry. [2]

    OGC CityGML 3.0 is probably the most important existing standard to study when designing Spatial IR. It is explicitly a platform-independent semantic information model for 3D urban objects, including geometry, relationships, multiple levels of detail, sensor/simulation data, and extensibility into domain-specific schemas. It is much closer philosophically to our IR than a mesh, point-cloud format, or USD scene alone. [3]

    NVIDIA Omniverse/Isaac Sim RTX LiDAR is the strongest match to the proposed forward-validation half of the architecture. Its simulated LiDAR can return not just coordinates but object IDs, material IDs, hit normals, channel/echo information, intensity, and other ground-truth attributes, allowing a known USD scene to produce richly labeled synthetic LiDAR. [4] This is almost exactly the forward operator needed for synthetic supervision and for testing whether a reconstructed scene can reproduce an observed scan.

    Cesium/3D Tiles is the strongest match for planetary-scale streaming and runtime metadata, rather than reconstruction. 3D Tiles can stream semantic metadata with massive heterogeneous geospatial geometry, while Cesium's current roadmap explicitly discusses semantics at multiple granularities and taxonomies applicable to object classes. [5]

    Esri CityEngine is the clearest existing analogue to the Theme/Geometry Compiler. Its CGA grammar system takes real or synthetic GIS geometry and procedurally generates architectural geometry, styles and textures; Esri explicitly positions it for rapid architectural-style changes and game-engine integration. [6] That means an important part of our architecture already has a mature precedent:

    structured spatial description+grammar→materialized world.

    The most consequential research gap is the combination of these capabilities. In the primary literature reviewed, I found substantial work on reconstruction, semantic segmentation, scene graphs, procedural city grammars, synthetic LiDAR, sim-to-real adaptation and streamed semantic 3D data—but not a mature city-scale system that reconstructs a hierarchical semantic world IR from LiDAR, materializes alternative themed worlds from it, and then uses a LiDAR forward simulator as an explicit reconstruction-consistency test. Neighboring work such as LiDARsim and learned LiDAR sensor models demonstrates that the forward half is technically credible. [7]

    Highest-priority actors and work

    
    MIT SPARK, Hydra and dynamic scene graphs

    Closest match to the Spatial IR concept itself.

    The 2022 RSS paper Hydra: A Real-time Spatial Perception Engine for 3D Scene Graph Construction and Optimization builds 3D scene graphs incrementally from sensor observations rather than treating the output as merely a point cloud or mesh. The broader SPARK work describes hierarchical spatial representations involving objects, places, rooms, buildings and relationships; the underlying Spark-DSG project is explicitly a data structure for dynamic scene graphs. Hydra and Spark-DSG are open-source under BSD-2-Clause. [1]

    That makes Hydra conceptually extremely close to:

    observations→geometry→semantic relational graph.

    Its mismatch with our project is scale and domain: Hydra is primarily designed for robotics and online embodied perception rather than country-scale airborne LiDAR. It also does not contain the proposed historical/cyberpunk materialization compiler. Architecturally, however, it is perhaps the single closest public implementation of the canonical world-graph layer.

    MIT SPARK's Khronos extends this idea into a spatio-temporal metric-semantic map that can reason over both short-term dynamics and long-term changes, which is directly relevant if Spatial IR eventually gets a time dimension rather than storing only a frozen world state. [8] The 2026 DAAAM project further combines SAM-style segmentation, tracking and vision-language grounding with Hydra to construct 3D Dynamic Scene Graphs online. [9]

    Primary sources: Hydra repository, Spark-DSG, SPARK publications. [10]

    One qualification: the closest match I found at MIT is SPARK, rather than a project that should specifically be described as a CSAIL project. For collaboration purposes I would target the SPARK/Hydra authors rather than choosing an MIT lab purely because it carries the CSAIL name. [11]

    
    
    TU Delft, City3D and the 3D geoinformation ecosystem

    Closest match to the airborne-LiDAR front end.

    City3D: Large-Scale Building Reconstruction from Airborne LiDAR Point Clouds by Huang, Stoter, Peters and Nan was published in 2022. It automatically reconstructs LoD2 building geometry from airborne LiDAR, detecting planar roof and wall structures and using structural/topological constraints to select a compact building model; the work explicitly addresses the missing façade geometry typical of airborne scans. [12] Its implementation is public under GPL-3.0-or-later. [13]

    TU Delft's broader ecosystem matters even more than City3D in isolation. Its 3D geoinformation work includes tools such as 3dfier, which lifts GIS polygons into 3D using elevation/point-cloud information, and 3D BAG, which automatically creates multiple levels of detail for Dutch buildings from national building footprints and elevation data. The group therefore has practical experience with exactly the multimodal fusion we would need:

    LiDAR+GIS footprints→structured city geometry.

    [14]

    City4CFD is also relevant because it turns terrain, point-cloud and urban feature information into simulation-ready city geometries rather than simply rendering point clouds. [15]

    Primary sources: City3D paper, City3D repository. [16]

    For our architecture, TU Delft represents the likely bridge between:

    raw geospatial observations and semantic Spatial IR.

    
    
    OGC CityGML and CityJSON

    Closest existing standard to a city-scale Spatial IR.

    CityGML 3.0 is explicitly described by OGC as a common semantic information model for 3D urban objects, defining both human interpretation and geometric representation and relationships. It is intentionally separated from one serialization format and can be encoded through multiple technologies. [17]

    It also supports multiple levels of detail and dynamic information originating from sensors and simulations. [18] CityJSON provides a JSON-oriented encoding of a subset of the CityGML model intended to make generation and programmatic manipulation easier. [19]

    The resemblance to Spatial IR is substantial:

    Spatial IR                  CityGML------------------------------------------------Building                    BuildingWall/roof components        Boundary surfacesObject hierarchy            Semantic city objectsGeometry alternatives       Multiple geometries / LoDsRelations                   Explicit model relationshipsExtensions                  Application Domain ExtensionsSerialization independence  Conceptual model + encodings

    The main difference is that CityGML is fundamentally an urban information model, not an inference engine. It tells us what a structured world can look like after reconstruction; it does not solve:

    LiDAR→CityGML semantics.

    That makes it a potentially excellent schema ancestor for Spatial IR rather than the complete implementation.

    Primary source: OGC CityGML standard. [20]

    
    
    NVIDIA Omniverse and RTX LiDAR

    Closest match to the proposed inverse/forward validation loop.

    NVIDIA's RTX LiDAR implementation takes a known USD/Omniverse world and simulates LiDAR returns. Its annotator interfaces can expose per-return objectId, materialId, hit normals, echo/channel information and other auxiliary measurements. An object ID can map a return back to the scene prim that produced it. [4]

    This means a synthetic training scene can provide labels that would be expensive or impossible to obtain from ordinary real-world LiDAR:

    pi→{object,material,surface normal,sensor channel,return/echo}.

    More importantly, it makes our proposed round trip viable:

    S→RTX LiDARP→Spatial CompilerS→RTX LiDARP.

    A reconstruction objective can then contain a measurement-consistency term such as

    $$
    \mathcal L_{\rm rescan}=D(P,\hat P).
    $$

    NVIDIA supplies the forward sensor model; our missing component is the inverse semantic world compiler.

    Primary source: Omniverse RTX LiDAR extension. [21]

    
    
    Esri CityEngine

    Closest match to the Theme + Geometry Compiler.

    CityEngine already treats geographic structure as input to a procedural architectural grammar. Esri describes it as a procedural 3D city generator operating on real or synthetic GIS data, with rapid changes to architectural style and textures. [6]

    That is remarkably similar to the downstream half of Spatial IR:

    semantic object+theme rule→generated geometry/assets/materials.

    CGA rules can progressively refine shapes into architecture, and CityEngine workflows can integrate with game-engine environments. [22]

    The distinction is that CityEngine generally begins with already-structured GIS/design primitives. Our proposed system introduces the missing upstream compiler:

    messy measured reality→structured semantic primitives→CityEngine-like grammar.

    Primary source: ArcGIS CityEngine. [6]

    
    
    Cesium and 3D Tiles

    Best match to the World Runtime / semantic streaming layer.

    3D Tiles is designed for streaming massive heterogeneous 3D geospatial datasets, and modern 3D Tiles supports streamed semantic metadata alongside geometry. [23] Cesium's own roadmap says the metadata architecture is intended to represent semantics at different granularities and notes the need for semantic vocabularies and object taxonomies. [24]

    Cesium also demonstrates hierarchical runtime selection and GPU-memory-aware loading. [25] This makes it a strong precedent for:

    Spatial IR→streaming tiles→engine runtime.

    What it does not currently provide is the inference pipeline that decides that a patch of LiDAR is a roof edge, wall corner, window boundary or supporting structure.

    CesiumJS and Cesium Native are available under Apache-2.0, and Cesium Native underlies integrations including Unreal, Unity and Omniverse. [26]

    Primary source: 3D Tiles Essentials. [27]

    
    
    ETH Zurich and Point2Building

    Important learned alternative to City3D's explicit geometric reconstruction.

    The 2024 Point2Building paper from ETH Zurich's Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing group learns to transform airborne LiDAR directly into polygonal building meshes. Its autoregressive model predicts sequences of vertices and faces and was evaluated on Zurich, Berlin and Tallinn. [28]

    Its architectural significance is that it challenges the assumption that our reconstruction layer must always be:

    points→plane detection→surface assembly.

    A Spatial Compiler could instead maintain multiple interchangeable reconstruction backends:

    explicit plane/topology inferenceCity3D-stylelearned direct mesh generationPoint2Building-styleimplicit/neural field reconstructionNeRF/SDF-style

    while forcing all three to emit the same Spatial IR.

    Primary sources: Point2Building paper, repository. [29]

    The repository is public; I did not find a sufficiently clear primary-source license statement in the material surfaced during this review, so its reuse license is marked unspecified rather than inferred. [30]

    
    
    Stanford 3D Scene Graph

    Stanford's 3D Scene Graph: A Structure for Unified Semantics, 3D Space, and Camera, published at ICCV 2019, is an important conceptual predecessor. It explicitly represents scene elements, attributes and relationships and uses registered imagery and 3D meshes to create a unified semantic-spatial representation. [31]

    Its scope is mainly indoor/embodied perception rather than aerial city reconstruction, but the conceptual resemblance to our world graph is strong:

    geometry+semantic entities+relations+observations.

    The project also released annotation and relationship-generation code. [31]

    Useful architecture/diagram source: Stanford 3D Scene Graph project page. [31]

    
    
    INRIA TITANE and Florent Lafarge

    INRIA's TITANE-related work is highly relevant to the multiscale geometry and reconstruction engine. Florent Lafarge describes his research as geometric modeling of 3D environments from physical measurements including multi-view imagery and laser scans, particularly surface reconstruction, city modeling, piecewise-planar geometry and spatial processes. [32]

    This is close to the layer that should decide:

    point neighborhood→ planar patch→ surface boundary→ intersection→ building component

    before a semantic model declares those structures to be a wall, roof or opening. INRIA work on compact urban meshes and combining geometric primitives with reconstructed surfaces is consequently more directly useful to our geometry engine than to the eventual theme compiler. [33]

    Primary source: Florent Lafarge / INRIA research page. [32]

    A notable industry trend is that commercial “digital twin” platforms still tend to produce a reality representation first—mesh, Gaussian splat, point cloud, BIM federation—and add semantics/workflows afterward. Bentley, for example, converts imagery and LiDAR into multiresolution meshes, splats, DSMs and point clouds and explicitly optimizes large models for streaming. [40] Hexagon similarly describes automated registration and meshing and now markets “digital realities” containing AI-extracted semantic information. [51] Neither publicly documents an ontology as deep as the proposed building → wall → corner → near-window → supports-roof Spatial IR.

    
    
    Adjacent research, validation and architectural gaps

    
    Open-vocabulary semantic understanding

    CitySeg (2025) is particularly relevant because it attacks a major weakness of fixed point-cloud classifiers: they normally require a predetermined class vocabulary. CitySeg aligns city-scale point-cloud features with text, introduces local-global cross-attention for sparse large-scale observations, and uses a hierarchical classification strategy across multiple urban datasets. [39]

    That makes a promising semantic front end:

    point/patch embedding+text ontology→open semantic label.

    But a label such as wall is still far from:

    Wall_103    part_of = Building_18    orientation = vertical    role = exterior    adjacent_to = Window_6    intersects = RoofPlane_2    boundary_type = convex_corner

    So CitySeg solves vocabulary, not world structure.

    The architecture should therefore treat semantic segmentation as evidence for graph construction, not as the final representation:

    segmentation≠Spatial IR

    
    
    Synthetic-to-real LiDAR and forward simulation

    There is a mature neighboring research thread demonstrating that synthetic LiDAR can be brought significantly closer to real sensors.

    LiDARsim (CVPR 2020) constructs static maps and dynamic-object catalogs from real driving data, ray-casts through the resulting 3D scene and uses a learned model to reproduce deviations from idealized physics; its target applications include perception testing and closed-loop safety evaluation. [52]

    Learning to Simulate Realistic LiDARs (IROS 2022) learns effects including ray dropout and intensity from paired appearance/LiDAR information, then applies those learned effects to naïvely ray-cast synthetic point clouds. [53]

    SynLiDAR / Point Cloud Translator (2021) tackles the synthetic-to-real domain gap by translating synthetic clouds in both appearance and sparsity dimensions. [54] Earlier work such as SqueezeSegV2 also used synthetic LiDAR combined with domain-adaptation techniques for real-world segmentation. [55] More recent research continues to focus on density translation and domain adaptation. [56]

    There is even prior work using CycleGAN-style mappings between synthetic and real bird's-eye-view point-cloud representations. [57]

    However, that is different from the cycle we are proposing.

    Existing cycle/domain-adaptation work generally resembles:

    $$
    P_{\rm synthetic}
    \leftrightarrow
    P_{\rm real}.
    $$

    Our proposed physical cycle is:

    $$
    P_{\rm real}
    \xrightarrow{\text{inverse reconstruction}}
    S_{\rm semantic}
    \xrightarrow{\text{forward sensor physics}}
    \hat P_{\rm real}.
    $$

    The latent variable S is not another point cloud. It is a structured world.

    That distinction appears important. In the primary sources reviewed, I did not find a city-scale semantic reconstruction system whose explicit objective is simultaneously:

    $$
    \mathcal L =
    \lambda_g\mathcal L_{\rm geometry}

    +
    \lambda_s\mathcal L_{\rm semantics}

    +
    \lambda_t\mathcal L_{\rm topology}

    +
    \lambda_r\mathcal L_{\rm rescan}.
    $$

    The forward technologies necessary for $\mathcal L_{\rm rescan}$ clearly exist in NVIDIA RTX LiDAR, LiDARsim and learned sensor-simulation research. [58] The opportunity is to connect them to an explicit semantic inverse model.

    
    
    Semantic LOD versus ordinary geometric LOD

    Several ecosystems already solve pieces of semantic LOD.

    CityGML permits urban objects to have multiple Levels of Detail. [59] The 3D BAG pipeline produces buildings at multiple LoDs. [60] Cesium/3D Tiles uses hierarchical selection and geometric error to determine what data to stream/render, while retaining feature metadata. [61] Potree uses octree-style level-of-detail structures for large point-cloud visualization. [62] OGC CDB similarly organizes simulation data into tiles, layers and LoDs for real-time clients. [50]

    The proposed Spatial IR could go one step further and make the semantic graph itself an LOD mechanism:

    $$
    \begin{aligned}
    100\,{\rm km}:&\quad \text{City}\\
    10\,{\rm km}:&\quad \text{Building}\\
    1\,{\rm km}:&\quad \text{Roof + walls}\\
    100\,{\rm m}:&\quad \text{windows + doors + trees}\\
    10\,{\rm m}:&\quad \text{trim + joints + surface details}.
    \end{aligned}
    $$

    That is different from simply reducing polygon count. Instead, the compiler changes the ontological resolution of the world. Current standards provide important ingredients, but I found no dominant city-streaming architecture built explicitly around this semantic-complexity principle.

    
    
    Materialization, geometry grammars and GIS-to-game compilation

    CityEngine is the strongest existing evidence that the downstream half of the concept is practical. Its procedural grammar can transform geographic shapes into architecture and rapidly modify architectural styles, parameters and textures. [63]

    Cesium provides the complementary runtime mechanism for bringing very large geospatial environments into engines; Cesium Native is used by official Unreal, Unity and Omniverse integrations. [64]

    This suggests a clean separation:

                        SPATIAL IR                        │             ┌──────────┴───────────┐             ▼                      ▼      Geometry Grammar        Runtime Compiler             │                      │     style / era / assets       tiles / paging             │                      │             └──────────┬───────────┘                        ▼                Unreal / WebGPU /              Omniverse / custom GPU

    The strategically interesting difference from CityEngine is the input. CityEngine begins with structured geographic objects; our system would attempt to discover those objects and their finer structural relations from observations first. [6]

    
    
    Multi-representation worlds

    Commercial reality-capture systems reinforce another part of our design: there is no need to force one geometric representation to serve every downstream use.

    Bentley can generate meshes, point clouds, Gaussian splats, DSMs and other outputs from the same source observations. [40] Open3D similarly exposes point clouds, voxel grids, octrees and triangle meshes as distinct representations. [65]

    That supports making a Spatial IR node capable of holding or referencing multiple representations:

    Oi={Pi, Vi, Mi, Gi, Θi},

    where

    Pi = measured point evidence,

    Vi = sparse occupancy/voxel representation,

    Mi = render mesh,

    Gi = semantic/topological graph state,

    Θi = parametric or procedural model.

    The semantic identity remains stable even if the geometry representation changes.

    
    
    
    Milestone timeline

    The timeline below shows how several mostly independent research streams have been converging toward something resembling a Spatial World Compiler.

    

    The chronology is supported by the Stanford ICCV 2019 project, MIT's 2020 Dynamic Scene Graph and 2022 Hydra publications, City3D 2022, Point2Building 2024, CitySeg 2025 and DAAAM 2026. [66] LiDARsim appeared in 2020 and the learned realistic LiDAR simulator in 2022. [7]

    
    
    What appears genuinely differentiated

    The landscape suggests that the defensible contribution should not be framed as “AI converts LiDAR into textured 3D.” Nearly every major component of that sentence already exists somewhere.

    The stronger research thesis is:

    compile physical observations into a persistent semantic-topological intermediate representation

    and make that IR the boundary between perception and world generation.

    Each side of that table has strong primary precedents. [67] The differentiation is the composition and IR boundary.

    A particularly important design feature that remains uncommon in the reviewed systems is making provenance a first-class graph property:

    Roof_8    geometry_source = observed_lidar    confidence = 0.98Wall_12    geometry_source = inferred_from_roof_and_footprint    confidence = 0.81Window_4    semantic_source = imagery_model    confidence = 0.67Quoin_2    source = VictorianTheme.geometryGrammar    status = generated

    The other potentially distinctive element is physical closure:

    P→S→P

    rather than only:

    P→S.

    The reconstructed semantic world would have to remain capable of producing measurements compatible with the measurements from which it was inferred. NVIDIA RTX LiDAR's rich synthetic outputs, LiDARsim's ray-casting-plus-learned sensor model and related sim-to-real work make that direction technically grounded rather than speculative. [58]

    
    
    Collaboration and study priorities

    
    TU Delft 3D Geoinformation — first contact

    Study/contact first for the reconstruction and geospatial-IR side.

    TU Delft has the most directly relevant combination of airborne LiDAR reconstruction, GIS fusion, structured buildings, LoD generation, CityGML expertise and open-source tooling among the groups reviewed. City3D, 3dfier, 3D BAG and City4CFD collectively cover far more of our proposed front-end pipeline than any single academic project. [69]

    The most useful collaboration question would not be “how do we reconstruct buildings?” but:

    How would you extend a City3D/3D BAG-style reconstruction pipeline so that surfaces, structural roles, adjacency, confidence and provenance survive as a persistent graph rather than being collapsed into final polygons?

    That directly tests the Spatial IR thesis.

    
    
    MIT SPARK — second contact

    Study/contact first for the graph ontology and runtime semantics.

    Hydra/Spark-DSG already treats 3D perception as the construction of a hierarchical relational graph rather than a mesh-generation problem. [34] MIT's later Khronos and DAAAM work shows the same architecture expanding toward temporal reasoning and foundation-model semantics. [70]

    The key research question for SPARK would be:

    Can a dynamic scene graph architecture designed for robotics be generalized into a geospatially indexed, out-of-core scene graph spanning blocks, cities and eventually national-scale datasets?

    Conceptually, that is very close to taking Hydra and Cesium and meeting in the middle.

    
    
    NVIDIA Omniverse / Isaac Sim RTX Sensors — third contact

    Study/contact first for synthetic supervision and measurable validation.

    NVIDIA already has the hardest piece of the proposed forward experiment: a programmable scene graph plus an RTX LiDAR sensor capable of revealing ground-truth object/material correspondence and hit geometry. [4]

    A collaboration prototype could therefore be extremely concrete:

    $$
    S_{\rm USD}
    \rightarrow
    P_{\rm RTX}
    \rightarrow
    \text{Spatial Compiler}
    \rightarrow
    \hat S
    \rightarrow
    \hat P_{\rm RTX}.
    $$

    The first benchmark would ask whether the compiler recovers not just semantic point classes but object identity, surfaces, edges, corners, hierarchy and relationships from scans of known synthetic worlds.

    That would give the project a research benchmark that few “LiDAR-to-mesh” systems possess: because $S_{\rm USD}$ is known exactly, we can independently measure geometry, topology, semantics and sensor reconstruction.

    For immediate technical study after those three, I would put OGC CityGML fourth, Esri CityEngine/CGA fifth, and Cesium/3D Tiles sixth. CityGML provides the strongest schema precedent, CityEngine the strongest procedural materialization precedent, and Cesium the strongest large-world delivery precedent. [71]

    Taken together, the research landscape supports a fairly specific architecture:

    USGS / sensors / GIS↓PDAL + Open3D-style geometry processing↓City3D / Point2Building-style reconstruction↓CitySeg / multimodal semantic inference↓Hydra-like graph + CityGML-like ontology = Spatial IR↓CityEngine-like geometry/material grammar↓Cesium / USD / Unreal / custom runtime↓RTX LiDAR forward validation

    Every box in that pipeline has a credible existing technical precedent. [72] What remains conspicuously unclaimed in the reviewed work is the engine-agnostic, provenance-aware semantic Spatial IR that connects all of them—and remains stable while geometry, materials, themes, engines and sensor representations are swapped around it.
